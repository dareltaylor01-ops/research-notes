## 1. Summary (5 bullets)

1. **All four seed gaps verify, but each needs a qualifier.** McKinsey's 37% means "at least some EBIT impact", not "positive EBIT," and it is flat on last year (McKinsey, https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai, 2026-08-25). Wharton's growth-metric edge is 35% vs 13%, which is 2.7x, not 3x (Wharton-GBK, https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Wharton-GBK-AI-Adoption-Report-2026.pdf, 2026-10-13).
2. **Surveys disagree sharply on whether AI pays.** McKinsey: 37% report some EBIT impact. Wharton-GBK: 80% of enterprise leaders report positive ROI, 28% strongly positive (GBK press release, https://www.gbkcollective.com/thoughtleadership/wharton-gbk-ai-study-2026-press-release, 2026-10-13). NBER executive survey: 89% report no labor-productivity impact over three years (NBER digest, https://www.nber.org/digest/202605/global-evidence-business-use-ai, 2026-05-01). The three use different questions, samples and time windows, so none can be picked as "the" number.
3. **"We can't see it in the productivity numbers" has been partly overtaken.** BLS reported Q3 2026 nonfarm productivity up 3.6% annualized, 2.1% year on year, the strongest quarter in a year (Axios, https://www.axios.com/2026/11/05/labor-output-productivity-ai, 2026-11-05; Reuters via Investing.com, https://www.investing.com/news/economic-indicators/us-worker-productivity-surges-in-third-quarter-5004543, 2026-11-05). Attribution to AI is contested: William Blair sees "still-limited evidence that this represents a broad AI-driven productivity boom" (Axios, 2026-11-05).
4. **The 515-startup experiment is real, but the headline 1.9x is statistically thin and what was randomized was information, not tools.** Kim, Kim and Koning report 1.9x revenue on a log(x+1) basis at p<0.1, while the raw revenue difference is negative and insignificant (paper, https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ssrn-6513481.pdf, 2026-03-30 version). The treatment was case studies of how other firms reorganized around AI.
5. **SC's "token bill is a rounding error" holds for the median firm only.** Ramp's Sep 2026 median firm paying for AI spends $15.70 per employee per month, but the top 1% spend a median $9,567.53 (Ramp, https://ramp.com/data/ai-index, data through 2026-09, checked 2026-11-09). McKinsey says about 20% of respondents are constrained by AI operating costs (2026-08-25).

## 2. What moved since last check (2026-10-26) and since SC publish (2026-09-25)

- **BLS Q3 productivity (2026-11-05):** 3.6% annualized; output +3.7%, hours +0.1%; unit labor costs −0.7%; labor share 53.0%, lowest since 1947 (BLS release via The Tribune/ANI, https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/us-labour-productivity-rises-3-6-in-q3-2026-unit-labour-costs-decline-0-7/, 2026-11-05). Q2 was revised up to 2.1% from 1.4% (same). BCA Research reads it as "the demand-side impact of the AI capex boom, not supply-side gains from ex-tech AI adoption" (BCA, https://www.bcaresearch.com/reports/us-productivity-reflects-strong-growth-not-efficiency-05-11-2026/227456, 2026-11-05; snippet only, paywalled).

AI share of business spend 5.77% vs 5.96% in August (Ramp index page). Sample is Ramp customers only and understates free-tool use (Ramp).
- **Wharton-GBK (2026-10-13):** already in the tracker. This run read the primary PDF and found the dose-response: strongly positive ROI is 13% for efficiency-only, 32% for one growth metric, 37% for two, 43% for three (n=65, authors flag as small) (Wharton-GBK PDF, 2026-10).

### Flag 2: Sourcing discrepancy
- **R3 (H) Does AI show up in the P&L?** Record all, pick none:
  - McKinsey: 37% attribute "at least some EBIT impact" to AI; about 6% are high performers (≥5% of EBIT and "significant" value); both flat vs 2025; 1,719 respondents, fielded 2026-05-04 to 2026-06-08 (McKinsey, 2026-08-25).
  - Wharton-GBK: 80% positive ROI, 28% strongly positive; 1,416 US respondents at firms with 1,000+ employees; fielded 2026-07-09 to 2026-07-29 (GBK press release and Beri summary, https://www.beri.net/article/wharton-gbk-ai-adoption-report-2026-growth-metrics-35-percent-strong-roi-vs-efficiency-13-chief-ai-officer-training-gap, 2026-10-13).
  - NBER/Fed/BoE/Bundesbank survey: 89% report no labor-productivity impact over three years; executives expect +1.4% over the next three; about 6,000 firms, fielded Nov 2025 to Jan 2026 (NBER digest, 2026-05-01).
  - Differences: ROI is self-reported and not the same as EBIT attribution, and the windows differ. Severity H because any "X% of firms see ROI" claim is definition-dependent. Verified.
- **R4 (M) Wharton "3x".** 35% ÷ 13% = 2.7x ("nearly 3x" per the press release, 2026-10-13). Seed's "3x" overstates slightly. Verified (primary PDF).
- **R5 (M) Ramp median spend.** SC: median company spends $12.50 per employee per month (SC, 2026-09-25, citing Ramp). Ramp's index page now shows the median firm paying for AI at $15.70 (Sep) and $14.74 (Aug) (Ramp, 2026-11-09). A Korean secondary note says Ramp chart data showed $12.50 for Aug 2026 (Learn.ai.kr, https://learn.ai.kr/notes/chamath-ai-roi-automation-coordination, 2026-10-05), so the difference may be a definition (all firms vs only firms paying) or a revision. Ramp primary for the $12.50 series not found: **UNVERIFIED**.

### Flag 3: Missing mechanism
- **R6 (M) Why growth metrics go with strong ROI.** SC does not cite Wharton. Wharton shows association only; the growth bucket is broad (output volume "e.g., lines of code, marketing campaigns, reports generated" counts as growth) and efficiency KPIs dominate use (hours saved 40%, net-new revenue 27%) (Wharton-GBK PDF, 2026-10). Reverse causality (firms already doing well measure growth) is not ruled out by the survey. Verified as a caveat; mechanism UNVERIFIED.
- **R7 (M) Organization capital as the mechanism.** NBER w35684: AI investment (measured by AI-skilled employment) is associated with productivity growth in recent years but not the prior decade, "traced" to organization capital (NBER, https://www.nber.org/papers/w35684, 2026-08). ITIF summary: a one-SD rise in AI-worker share growth goes with +6.2 pp sales-per-worker growth and +7.5 pp TFP growth over six years (2018–2024), about 1 pp a year; 58% of AI jobs build organization capital vs 31% of non-AI jobs (ITIF, 2026-09-08). Primary PDF numbers not read, so the magnitudes are verified via ITIF only. Observational, not causal.
- **R8 (M) The experiment's mechanism is "mapping."** Treated firms found 2.7 more AI use cases (+44%), completed 12% more tasks, were 11 pp (18%) more likely to get paying customers, and cut capital demand 39.5% with labor unchanged (Kim, Kim, Koning, 2026-03-30). All firms already had the same AI tools, training and credits; the randomized treatment was information on how others reorganized. SC's "same tools, different reorganization" framing matches the design. Verified (primary).

### Flag 4: Missing counter-evidence
- **R9 (H) Revenue effect is statistically fragile.** Paper: 1.9x on log(x+1), coefficient 0.65, p<0.1; 2.2x conditional on earning revenue (p<0.05). Appendix Table A3 shows the raw revenue effect at −1,747,177 (SE 1,861,794) on a control mean of 2,101,176, which is insignificant and wrong-signed, so the result depends on transformation and outliers; at 90th-percentile winsorization the effect is 3,728 on 16,205 (units as printed; not confirmed) (paper, 2026-03-30). SC's "enough to push its revenue to 1.9x the control group" omits this.
- **R10 (M) SC's "almost all gains from the top 10%" vs the paper.** Paper: gains "largest at the 90th percentile and above," but also "no significant differential effects by baseline firm performance or founder technical background" (paper, 2026-03-30). SC's concentration claim is directionally supported; "almost all" is SC's wording, not the paper's. External validity: median firm founded 2024 with a team of four; sample 33.8% Middle East and Africa; median monthly revenue among revenue-generating firms $1,000 at baseline (paper).
- **R11 (M) Individual gains vs firm gains.** McKinsey: 80% say AI improved their own productivity, yet EBIT attribution is flat at 37% (McKinsey, 2026-08-25). 14% report an actual workforce decline vs 32% who expected one a year earlier (McKinsey). NBER exec survey: employees expect productivity +0.9% vs executives' +2.3% (NBER digest, 2026-05-01).

### Flag 5: Single-constraint framing
- **R12 (M) "Humans are the bottleneck."** SC names coordination and meetings (SC, 2026-09-25). Public sources add stacked constraints: discovery/mapping (Kim et al., 2026-03-30); measurement choice (Wharton, 2026-10-13); AI operating costs for about 20% of firms (McKinsey, 2026-08-25); training gap, with 78% of leaders vs 56% of entry/mid-level staff saying training is adequate (GBK press release, 2026-10-13); and workflow redesign, done by about three-quarters of high performers vs one-quarter of others (McKinsey, 2026-08-25).

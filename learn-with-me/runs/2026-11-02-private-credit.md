## 1. Summary (5 bullets)

1. **SC's sharpest number, "89 to 91 cents" for Project Jupiter, is already stale.** SC cited Jupiter's $18B financing at 89–91 cents (SC, https://research.socialcapital.com/p/private-credit, 2026-10-09). The New York Times later reported pieces trading at about 80 cents, with the air-permit hearing for the site's microgrid pushed to Dec 7 (NYT as relayed by FinancialMarkets.com, https://www.financialmarkets.com/article/debt-on-oracles-new-mexico-ai-campus-has-traded-near-80-cents-a-month-ago-it-was, 2026-10-19). Oracle had already served a force-majeure notice (Bloomberg headline, https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/oracle-cites-force-majeure-to-shield-itself-on-controversial-data-center, 2026-09-24). The NYT original was not read, so this is verified only through a secondary source.
2. **The BIS "6.2 vs 6.1 points" spread finding is a January 2026 snapshot, and market pricing has since separated.** BIS found AI-related private loans priced at 6.2 pp against 6.1 pp for other private credit (BIS Bulletin 120, https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull120.pdf, 2026-01-07). By October, at least 18 data-center financings carried double-digit yields, and the top-rated data-center bonds had gone from below 5% to about 7% (Bloomberg via swissinfo, https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/wall-streets-ai-debt-machine-is-starting-to-choke-on-supply/92288022, 2026-10-28). Volta's $5B loan cleared at 95 cents and SOFR+650 bp, for a yield above 11% (Bloomberg via The Business Times, https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/banking-finance/jpmorgan-goldman-boost-yield-volta-ai-loan-lure-investors, 2026-10-16).
3. **SC's CoreWeave maturity-mismatch figure conflicts with CoreWeave's own filing.** SC says CoreWeave "borrowed for five years against customer contracts averaging about three" (SC, 2026-10-09). CoreWeave's 10-K says its "committed contracts had a weighted-average contract duration of approximately five years" as of Dec 31, 2025 (CoreWeave 10-K FY2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000104/crwv-20251231.htm). SC's basis is in the paywalled deck: **UNVERIFIED (attribution)**.
4. **SC's redemption claim is verified, but the trend may have turned.** In Q2 2026 outflows beat inflows at non-traded BDCs for the first time since at least 2022: $3.3B of new shares against $8.1B paid out across 27 funds (PitchBook, https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/record-redemptions-low-fundraising-squeeze-private-credit-funds-pitchbook, 2026-10-12). In Q3, requests eased to 11.5% of NAV from 12.7% and the unmet backlog fell to $8.2B from $9.8B. Stanger said demand "may have peaked in the second quarter" (Stanger via AltsWire, https://altswire.com/nontraded-bdc-redemption-requests-decrease-in-q3-as-backlog-falls-to-8-2b/, 2026-10-02).
5. **The LSEG circular-finance map, UNVERIFIED last run because of a 404, is now verified, and it reframes the risk.** FTSE Russell calls AI financing "less 'circular' and more of a web" and says it is "not 2008." Its closer analogue is the late-1990s telecom overbuild (LSEG/FTSE Russell, https://www.lseg.com/en/insights/ftse-russell/mapping-circular-financing-in-ai-does-it-add-Instability, 2026-10-21; map data as at 2026-08-18).

## 2. What moved since last run (2026-10-26)

- **Hyperion recovered slightly.** The Beignet 6.581% 2049 notes last traded at 92.94 on Oct 30, a 7.22% yield to maturity and +202 bp over Treasuries (FINRA TRACE via Kilambi, https://news.kilambi.com/leverage/bond/u06930aa6/, accessed 2026-11-02). Last run's figure was "a little under 91 cents" (FT via Yahoo, 2026-10-15). Kilambi is a single third-party aggregator and the bond trades thinly (2 prints on Oct 30), so treat this as indicative.

  - more than 30 financiers said raising money is "significantly more difficult"
  - data-center ABS and CMBS issuance is about $22B this year, "barely keeping pace with last year"
  - the IG tech index yields about 6%, up 1.4 pp year on year
  - CDS on Oracle, SpaceX, Nvidia and Meta hit records "last week"

Moody's expects capex by the five largest US hyperscalers plus CoreWeave to reach "nearly $1 trillion in 2027" (Moody's via Reuters/Investing.com, https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/ai-financing-driving-private-credit-growth-as-refinancing-risks-rise-moodys-says-4976254, 2026-10-27).

A keyword search of the transcript found no mention of "Jupiter," so no sponsor mark on Jupiter was disclosed (Blue Owl Q3 2026 call transcript, https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/29/blue-owl-owl-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/, 2026-10-29).

### Flag 3: Missing mechanism
- **P8 (M) Why "little extra for AI risk" can coexist with stress: pricing follows the tenant, not "AI."** CoreWeave's own facilities show the spread:
  - DDTL 4.0 ($8.5B): SOFR+2.25%
  - DDTL 5.0 ($3.1B): SOFR+4.50%
  - 9.75% 2031 notes: unsecured

  (CoreWeave 10-Q Q2 2026, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000366/crwv-20260630.htm). Reuters reports DDTL 4.0 is rated A3 "largely because lenders rely on Meta's contractual payments" (Reuters, https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/nvidias-bet-that-its-chips-can-finance-ai-boom-gets-wall-street-reality-check-2026-10-01/, 2026-10-01; search snippet only, fetch 401). Likewise, Volta's banks priced the $3.7B chip loan within a single deal that also holds a $1.3B lease term loan (Business Times/Bloomberg, 2026-10-16). An average spread across "AI" hides the credit stack. SC's public excerpt hints at this ("two AI financings depended on tenant payments… Why?") but answers it only in the paywalled deck.
- **P9 (M) Off-ramps and force majeure are the transmission channel.** One hedge fund passed on a deal after finding the customer could exit after a delay of more than three months. Lawyers have seen 180-day walk-away clauses (Bloomberg/swissinfo, 2026-10-28). Oracle's Jupiter force-majeure notice can extend the period before full rent (FinancialMarkets/NYT, 2026-10-19). SC's public text covers maturity mismatch but not contractual exit rights.
- **P10 (M) Refinancing wall and PIK.** These are the mechanism by which "AI takes longer to pay off" (SC) becomes credit loss: loans due 2027–2029, with 17% of 607 borrowers on PIK (Moody's/Reuters, 2026-10-27).

### Flag 4: Missing counter-evidence
- **P11 (M) Not 2008.** LSEG finds no public evidence of "synthetic replication or complex derivative chains," and bank lending is "relatively limited" (LSEG, 2026-10-21). UBS: "There may never be a single point of failure… the clearest credit risks ultimately lead back to the research labs," and scarce compute "creates a firmer near term base" (UBS, 2026-09-29). Both cut against a pure bubble reading. SC's own framing is open-ended (a question), so this is context, not a rebuttal.
- **P12 (L) Wealth-channel stabilization.** See P3 (Stanger 2026-10-02; Blue Owl 2026-10-29).

### Flag 5: Single-constraint framing
- **P13 (M) Not just a "maturity mismatch."** SC's public excerpt centers on maturity mismatch (CoreWeave 5 vs 3; Hyperion 2049 vs 4-year leases). Evidence shows a stack of constraints:
  - duration and rates: the Hyperion fall was attributed to the long-Treasury selloff and new supply (Seeking Alpha search summary, 2026-10-07)
  - power and permits: Jupiter (FinancialMarkets/NYT, 2026-10-19)
  - contract off-ramps (Bloomberg, 2026-10-28)
  - lender and insurer exposure caps, with about 5 months to offload risk (Bloomberg, 2026-10-28)
  - refinancing wall and PIK (Moody's, 2026-10-27)

## Summary (from the video description and auto-generated transcript)
Groupthink happens when agreement starts to feel like evidence. The video gives 12 reasons polymaths resist it:
1. Too many mental frameworks for one narrative to own them.
2. They notice when a group confuses agreement with truth ("consensus is information, but it is not evidence by itself").
3. Curiosity keeps pulling in sources from outside the group's frame.
4. They can be outsiders without feeling intellectually lost, because their interests and sense of belonging span more than one group.
5. They spot the same pattern across unrelated fields.
6. They distrust ideas that lean on how many people believe them.
7. They look for hidden assumptions before accepting a conclusion.
8. Their identity is spread across several domains, so being wrong doesn't feel like betrayal.
9. They've been wrong in enough fields that they don't worship certainty.
10. They see the social incentives behind opinions.
11. They separate "this is familiar" from "this is true."
12. They look for the missing variable.

Closing claim: "the deepest protection against groupthink" is "the ability to keep thinking after everyone else has decided the thinking is finished."

## Caveat
"Completely immune" is the title's framing, not a proven fact. The video itself hedges: polymaths "can be remarkably resistant," are "not naturally immune to loneliness or social pressure," and "constant disagreement is not independent thinking." The mechanism it describes is having more independent reference points to check a narrative against. It cites "research on group decision-making" in general terms but names no studies. Polymaths can still fall for groupthink.
